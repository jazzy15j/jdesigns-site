Big brands are already running AI ads. Here's what they're actually doing (and getting wrong).

I caught a Tommy Hilfiger ad on Snapchat recently that stopped my scroll — it had that unmistakable AI-made feel. Turns out that's not a coincidence. Tommy Hilfiger is one of Snapchat's earliest brand partners on AI ad formats: AI Sponsored Snaps, AI-powered Dynamic Product Ads, and autoplay video ads with a built-in chatbot. This is a confirmed, active partnership — not a one-off experiment. And once you notice it in one brand's ads, it starts showing up everywhere.

Who's actually running AI in their ads right now

  • Coca-Cola — Partnered with OpenAI and Bain & Company on "Create Real Magic," letting consumers generate their own Coca-Cola visuals with AI tools. Also used fully AI-generated video for its 2024 and 2025 holiday commercials.
  • Mango — Ran an ad campaign generated entirely with AI for its Teen line's Sunset Dream collection, across 95 markets.
  • H&M — Built AI-generated "digital twins" of 30 real models (with their consent) to produce hundreds of product images in different poses and styles without repeat photoshoots.
  • Cadbury (India) — Used AI facial mapping so Bollywood star Shah Rukh Khan appears to personally endorse thousands of individual local shops by name — one campaign, tens of thousands of personalized micro-ads.
  • Netflix — Its ad platform now generates modular AI ad placements that blend into the visual style of specific shows as you're watching them.
  • Lay's — Used AI to generate regionally personalized versions of its "Smile" campaign at a speed traditional production can't match.
  • Sprite — Ran a personalized AI avatar campaign where the avatar's script changed based on the viewer's activity and time of day.
  • Toys "R" Us — Made a full 66-second TV commercial with OpenAI's Sora, telling Geoffrey the Giraffe's origin story entirely in AI-generated video.

It's not all landing well

This is the part worth paying attention to. Coca-Cola's AI Christmas ads got called "soulless" two years running, with real boycott threats — because Christmas is the most nostalgic, human part of that brand, and AI felt like a betrayal of it. Toys "R" Us got the same reaction: "creepy," not magical. Big budgets and cutting-edge tools didn't protect either brand from a bad read on their own audience.

That's the actual lesson — not "AI ads flopped." The brands that used AI to do something only AI could do — Cadbury's thousands of personalized shop mentions, H&M cutting reshoot costs while keeping their actual models' consent — landed fine. The brands that used AI to replace the human, emotional core of what people already loved about them got called out for it immediately.

The adoption numbers are real

This isn't a handful of headline-chasing brands. Industry data puts AI-in-ad-creative adoption among top advertisers somewhere between 67% and 90% depending on how it's measured — up sharply from around 55% just a year ago. AI in advertising has moved from novelty to default this fast.

What this actually means for a small business

You don't need a Sora subscription or an OpenAI partnership to take something useful from this. What you need is the same discipline these brands are (mostly) missing:

  • AI is a tool, not a personality transplant. Use it to move faster on things like image variations, product mockups, or caption drafts — not to replace the parts of your brand that are actually you.
  • Know what your audience actually wants before you automate it. Coca-Cola's mistake wasn't using AI — it was using AI on the one part of their brand where people wanted human warmth, not efficiency.
  • Speed is the real advantage, not magic. H&M and Lay's used AI to produce more variations, faster — not to invent a strategy they didn't already have.

The takeaway isn't "start running AI ads because Coca-Cola and Tommy Hilfiger are." It's that even brands with unlimited budgets are still learning the same lesson small businesses need to learn: AI works when it's a tool applied with intention, and it backfires when it's used to skip the thinking. That's true whether your ad budget has nine zeros or three.
