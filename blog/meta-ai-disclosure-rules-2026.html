Does your Meta ad actually need an AI disclosure label?

Meta's AI content rules split into two very different tracks — one for political and social-issue ads, one for everyone else. Most "how to disclose AI in your Meta ads" guides blur the two together. Here's which one actually applies to your business.

The two-track system most guides get wrong

Meta's mandatory, penalty-backed AI disclosure rule is specific to ads about social issues, elections, or politics. If that kind of ad contains a photorealistic AI-generated or altered image, video, or realistic-sounding audio — a real person shown saying or doing something they didn't, a realistic-looking person or event that doesn't exist, or altered footage of something that actually happened — the advertiser has to disclose it, or the ad gets rejected. Meta has run automated detection backing this since June 1, 2026.

For a standard commercial ad — a product photo, a service ad, a UGC-style video — there's no equivalent manual step. Meta applies its own automatic "AI info" label when its own generative tools were used, or when its detection flags photorealistic third-party AI content. There's no toggle to find and no disclosure to add yourself. If you sell a product or service and your ad isn't about a social issue, election, or candidate, you're almost certainly on this track.

What Meta's ad review actually looks at

Meta's ad review system — which they refer to internally as the Andromeda system — reviews not just your ad copy but your images, video, audio, and even the landing page your ad links to. AI detection is part of that same review, looking for patterns in generated images and audio that differ from real photography or recordings.

The rejection-and-strike risk is real — but only on the political/social-issue track. If your ad is about a social issue, election, or candidate, skipping the required disclosure gets the ad rejected outright, and repeated failures affect your account standing. A standard product or service ad with AI-touched creative isn't operating under that rule — Meta's automatic label there isn't a strike against you.

So do you actually need to do anything?

If you run standard commercial ads: no. There's no setting to find and no label to add — Meta's own detection and labeling happens on its side. Spending time hunting for a "disclosure toggle" in Ads Manager for a product ad is time you don't need to spend.

If you run political, election, or social-issue ads: yes — Meta already requires advertiser authorization and disclaimer setup for this category, and photorealistic AI content adds an active disclosure requirement on top of that. This is a genuinely stricter, separate process from anything a standard business advertiser deals with.

What about AI tools inside Canva or other platforms?

The source tool doesn't change anything for Meta's detection — Canva's AI generator, Adobe Firefly, Midjourney, whatever you used, Meta evaluates the final creative, not where it came from. For a standard commercial ad, that just means: if the result reads as photorealistic AI content, Meta may apply its automatic label. There's still nothing for you to configure.

With Canva Grow 2.0 now able to publish straight to Meta, the same rule applies whether you build the ad in Ads Manager or push it from Canva.

AI voiceovers work the same way. If you used ElevenLabs, Murf, Descript, or a similar tool to generate a realistic narration, Meta's detection can flag that too — but for a standard commercial ad, that's still Meta's automatic label, not a manual disclosure step on your end.

The bottom line

AI creative is fine. For nearly every business running Meta ads, there's genuinely nothing to configure for AI disclosure — Meta handles its own labeling, and it doesn't hurt your ad's approval or performance. The rule worth actually worrying about only applies if your ad touches a social issue, election, or political topic. If that's not you, this isn't something to spend time on.
