Retargeting is not a switch you turn on

Retargeting shows ads to people who already know your business. But that simple definition skips the most important question: where did those people come from?

Meta says a retargeting audience may come from website visitors tracked through the Pixel, product or cart activity, app activity, Facebook or Instagram engagement, video viewers, a properly collected customer list, a shop, or another trackable Meta surface.

If the source does not exist—or it is too small, old, incorrectly tracked, or unrelated to the offer—there is no meaningful warm audience waiting behind the retargeting button.

The funnel is a sequence, not three campaign names

Top of funnel: discovery

The person is new. The campaign may use awareness, reach, video views, engagement, or qualified traffic to introduce the right people to a useful message and begin gathering signals.

Middle of funnel: consideration

The person has interacted but has not completed the primary action. They may have watched a video, visited the site, engaged with the business, or explored a product. The message can now educate, answer objections, show proof, compare options, or demonstrate the offer.

Bottom of funnel: higher intent

The person has taken an action closer to conversion, such as viewing a key product, starting checkout, completing part of a lead process, or returning repeatedly. Tracking, buying cycle, audience volume, and destination readiness determine whether this layer is useful.

Why a new campaign needs stability

Meta's Performance 5 guidance recommends simplified campaign structures, fewer unnecessary learning-phase changes, deliberate automation, diversified creative, stronger data quality, and controlled testing.

That is why optimization should not mean touching every setting every day. Constant restructuring can fragment the budget and make it harder to understand what caused a change.

Optimization and construction are different jobs

Ongoing optimization
Review performance and document decisionsMaintain
Refine budget or audiences inside the existing structureMaintain
Pause a true underperformer when evidence supports itMaintain
New campaign construction
Create Custom Audiences and inclusion/exclusion rulesNew build
Write stage-specific copy and produce/select creativeNew build
Build, verify, launch, and document a new funnel layerNew build

Maintaining the engine is recurring work. Adding a new section to the engine is a build.

What should exist before retargeting?

Confirm the audience source, recency, usable volume, tracking confidence, stage-appropriate message, working destination, and necessary exclusions. If those questions cannot be answered, the next step may be tracking repair, a discovery campaign, a better destination, or more time—not a rushed retargeting build.

What if the business already has data?

Then it may be possible to move faster. A new client may already have website traffic, product views, purchase events, engagement, video audiences, or a compliant customer list. The right approach is to audit what exists, verify it, and begin at the first missing layer.

The real best practice

1
Confirm the offer and destination.
2
Audit the account and tracking.
3
Identify the audience data that already exists.
4
Match the campaign to the audience's real stage.
5
Keep the structure simple enough to learn.
6
Build the next layer only when evidence supports it.

Retargeting is powerful because the audience already has context. But that context has to come from somewhere.
