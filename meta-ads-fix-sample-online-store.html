Online store: What happened to your ads, and what to do next

Sample report · Real audit of a small online accessories store · Business name, product details and account IDs removed · Data window Sep 2023 to Oct 2026

Verdict: NEEDS OPTIMIZATION.

Your account is healthy and your tracking works. What changed is how much each ad impression turns into a sale. Your four main ads are about three years old, and the cost of getting a purchase went from under $4 to over $10 while the cost of reaching people moved far less. That points to the ads and the offer people land on, not to your account setup.

Everything below comes from your Meta account data and the numbers you sent me. Profit is covered in Section 7, using the product cost, postage, and order numbers you sent. Nothing here guarantees a result; Meta results depend on your product, price, creative, and budget.

1. The short version

  1. Your ads worked very well for a long time. From Sep 2023 to mid-2024, a purchase cost about $2.70 to $4.40 and Meta-reported return was 3 to 4.8x.
  2. They did not stop working overnight. Cost per purchase stepped up three times: Jul-Aug 2024, again through 2025, and again from Aug 2026. By Sep 2026 it was $13.69, and $19.24 on Oct 1-3.
  3. Reaching people did not get nearly as expensive as buying got. Cost per 1,000 views rose about 13% ($4.12 in your best period to $4.65 in 2026), but cost per purchase rose about 2.8x ($3.70 to $10.51). In plain terms: about 60% fewer purchases per 1,000 views than in your best period (1.11 vs 0.44).
  4. Tracking is not the problem. Purchase tracking is strong, with one small cleanup (Section 5).
  5. The biggest levers are creative and the offer. Four ads carried about 99% of $191k in spend, all static white-background catalog images with a discount-led message.
  6. Your break-even cost per purchase is about $12, and your ads ran at about $14-15. With ads off, orders fell from about 22 a day to about 10, so the ads were driving roughly half your sales, at a small loss per order. That gap is closable with new creative and bigger orders (Section 7).

  • OKPayment method active, recent charges paid, no failed payments, no restrictions or disapprovals showing. An unbilled balance of $190.73 will charge automatically.
  • FIXNo URL parameters (UTM tags) on the ads I checked, so Shopify or any analytics tool can't tell which campaign a visit came from.

Structure

  • Everything runs as an Advantage+ catalog sales campaign with Advantage+ audience and automatic placements. That is a reasonable setup, but it leaves almost nothing but creative and offer for you to control.
  • 26 ads are listed. Only four ever carried meaningful spend: the original product ad (3 years old), the auto-created Mini Shop ad (about 3 years), Holiday Ad (about 2 years), and Holiday Ad 2025.
  • Typical "restart" pattern: several new ad sets at once in Aug-Sep 2026. Frequent resets make it hard to learn what is working.

Audience and placements

  • Every placement (Facebook feed, Instagram feed, Reels, Stories) got more expensive together. It is not a bad-placement problem, and turning placements off would save very little.
  • The audience got older over time: 18-34 was about 87% of spend early on, and ages 45+ were under 1%. In 2026, 45+ received about 20% of spend at weaker returns (ROAS 1.4-2.0). This is a modest drag, not the main cause.
  • Your best 2026 segment was women 18-34 (cost per purchase about $9.20-$9.60, ROAS 2.17) on only about 17% of spend. That is a lead worth testing, not proof.
  • Current targeting is broad (US, 18-65+, Advantage+) with suggestions for lookalikes of past buyers, brand interests, and a 180-day purchaser exclusion.

Creative and copy

  • Every ad is a catalog collection ad with white-background product images. No video, no lifestyle shots, no product in hand or in use, no customer content.
  • Meta itself shows a recommendation in your account: allowing product video could lower cost per result (Meta estimates 17%). That is Meta's estimate, not a promise.
  • Copy leans on discounts: a standing 15% code, plus 20% off 2 or 30% off 3+ in the sale ads. The sale ads were your weakest: $13.58 to $36.72 per purchase. Discounts cut the amount you keep per order without raising order size.
  • No hook, no proof (reviews), no product-style callout, and no mention of shipping.
  • In 2024 you ran ads for four specific product styles. They performed as well as or better than the generic ads, then were never scaled. Your messages also show customers asking for specific styles.

6. Priority recommendations

IMMEDIATE 1Set your break-even number before any new spend

What's wrong: At your $14.01 average order, a $13.69 cost per purchase (about $15.40 if duplicate Purchase events are inflating it) is above what each order earns you. Cost per purchase took about 98% of merchandise revenue in Sep 2026.

What to do: Work out contribution per order = average order value minus product cost minus payment fees minus any postage you pay beyond what you charge. That number is your maximum cost per purchase. Use it as the line for deciding whether a campaign stays or goes.

Your number: about $12 per purchase, using your numbers (Section 7). Any campaign that stays above that is losing money on first orders. A target of $9-10 leaves about $2-3 per order.

Why it matters: It gives you one line to judge every campaign against, instead of guessing.

IMMEDIATE 2Replace the four-ad creative set with a test system

What's wrong: About 99% of $191k went through four static, discount-led catalog ads, up to three years old.

What to do: Build a small batch of new creative, one idea per ad: (a) a short vertical video of the product in real use, (b) style-specific ads for your top 4-5 product styles, revisiting your 2024 winners, (c) a reviews or customer-photo ad, (d) a clear "ships in X days, flat shipping over $30" line in the copy. Drop the blanket percent-off headlines.

Expected effect: Gives Meta new things to learn from and tells you which angle earns purchases. It doesn't guarantee a result.

IMMEDIATE 3Raise order size, so each sale is worth more

What's wrong: Most orders are one item at $12.99, so there is little room to pay for ads. The 20-30% off ads lowered the realized order value and performed worst.

What to do: Test bundles or multi-packs (for example a 2-pack or 3-pack at a set price) and a free-shipping threshold set a little above your usual single-item order. Put those offers in the ads. This is a change to the offer, not a site redesign.

Why it matters: A higher average order is the main way to widen the gap between what an ad costs and what you keep. Because an item costs you about $1, a second item in the same order adds roughly $11 of contribution. At two items per order your break-even cost per purchase is about $22 instead of $12 (before any bundle discount).

NEXT 30 DAYS 1Clean up tracking

Keep one pixel app (Shopify's Facebook & Instagram channel, which sends server events) and remove or turn off the second app. Then place one real test order and confirm in Events Manager > Test events that a single Purchase fires. Add UTM parameters to every ad, for example utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid&utm_campaign={{campaign.name}}&utm_content={{ad.name}}. Improve server-side AddToCart coverage with the Shopify channel's data-sharing setting set to Maximum.

NEXT 30 DAYS 2Rebuild with fewer moving parts

When you decide to run ads again: one campaign, two ad sets at most, and leave each test alone for 2-3 weeks. Do not launch five ad sets at once or edit a winner. Test with and without a cost cap once new creative is in, since the $9 cap was not the cause of the decline.

NEXT 30 DAYS 3Test your best-looking audience

Give women 18-34 and related interests their own ad set so you can see whether the 2026 results hold. Keep a lookalike of past purchasers as a second test.

WHEN READY 1Plan the Q4 push early

In Q4 2024 and 2025 you spent $22k and $27k at near-normal returns, so seasonal volume exists. Have the new creative approved and the break-even number set before the holiday window, and plan any holiday offer around bundles.

WHEN READY 2Retargeting and Etsy

A small retargeting ad set for visitors and cart abandoners, and a way to tell how much of your Etsy volume follows Meta traffic. Etsy doesn't send purchase data to Meta, so those sales won't appear in Ads Manager.
